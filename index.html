Where to get the data

Be careful with "AI trading" sites, bots and signal groups. No one can guarantee profits. Avoid anyone who promises fixed returns, asks you to send money to a person, or wants your broker password. Check that an adviser is registered with your regulator (SEBI, FCA, SEC).

How much can I safely buy?

Enter your total money, the % you accept to lose on one trade (many people use 1%), your entry price and stop-loss price.

Before you invest

  • Only use money you can afford to lose. Keep an emergency fund first.
  • Signals use price history only. Also read the company's results, debt and news (links on every share).
  • A common rule of thumb: keep any one share under about 10% of your money and spread across sectors.
  • Use a stop-loss and decide it before you buy.
  • Fees, brokerage and tax reduce profits. Frequent trading costs more.
  • Be wary of tips on WhatsApp or Telegram and promises of guaranteed returns.
  • Beginners may prefer low-cost index funds. A registered adviser (SEBI in India, FCA in the UK) can advise on your situation.

What the words mean

  • Moving average: the average price over 20 or 50 days. Above it means recent strength.
  • RSI: 0 to 100. Under 30 is oversold (may bounce), over 70 overbought (may fall).
  • MACD: compares fast and slow trends. Positive means upward momentum.
  • Bollinger bands: a normal price range. Outside it means unusually high or low.
  • Stop-loss: a price where you sell to limit your loss. Target: where you may take profit.
  • Score: how many indicators agree. More agreement is not a guarantee.

Broker and alerts

How signals are made

Each share is scored from −7 to +7 using: price vs 50-day average, 20/50-day trend, RSI, 20-day momentum, MACD and Bollinger bands. Score 3 or more = BUY (with a suggested stop-loss and target from recent volatility), −3 or less = SELL, otherwise HOLD. This is rule-based maths on price history only; it does not read news, results or company fundamentals.
